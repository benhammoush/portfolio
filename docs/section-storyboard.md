## Narrative and composition contract

**Introduction → Engineering workflow → Apollo → Iris → Crypto gateway → Contact.**

The second Apollo entry was explicitly removed. Dripperz is not a homepage chapter
in this order; this does not silently cancel its separately specified case study.
Crypto gateway is the requested chapter name. Its current product narrative must
come from the active gateway contracts/evidence, not automatically inherit the
legacy 0x Shopify/SOL Devnet story.

The progression is identity → method → three project stories → conversation.
Workflow explains how to evaluate the projects; Apollo then leads the project
sequence. Avoid repeating the workflow explanation in every project chapter.

Use one continuous full-viewport world composed with HTML, not a sequence of
bordered canvases or identical text-left/object-right slides. Reserve clear space
for reading in every view. Change composition to support the narrative, not merely
to demonstrate camera movement. Native links/scroll and static/reduced-motion
alternatives must preserve meaning without waiting for animation.

## 01 — Introduction

**Takeaway:** Guillaume Ammouche is a full-stack developer who connects interfaces,
backends and reliable delivery.

- **Content:** name, professional role, one clear positioning sentence, an invitation
  to explore and a direct email action. React/TypeScript and Python/FastAPI support
  that positioning, rather than becoming the headline.
- **Candidate headline:** “From interface to production.”
- **Composition:** a strong typographic opening in the world, with enough negative
  space to read immediately. Establish the overall visual language, not the Iris
  installation as the universal homepage hero. Navigation remains visible and usable.
- **Transition:** carry the visitor from what is built to how it is built, without a
  loading gate or forced pause.
- **3D's eventual job:** establish a coherent space and visual identity. A literal hub
  sculpture is not required; no object is selected by this storyboard.
- **Evidence boundary:** identity, skills and email come from the owner-supplied CV.
  Do not introduce client logos, delivery totals or invented impact metrics.

## 02 — Engineering workflow

**Takeaway:** engineering judgment, explicit verification and human responsibility
connect a requirement to a delivered result.

- **Content:** one compact, readable progression:
  1. **Define:** outcome, specification, constraints and acceptance criteria.
  2. **Build and verify:** bounded implementation, relevant checks and independent review.
  3. **Accept and deliver:** human acceptance, exact-source publication/release and recovery.
- **Supporting detail:** AI-assisted tools can help exploration and bounded
  implementation; they do not own architecture, acceptance or release decisions.
  Do not lead the section with an AI brand, agent diagram or tool-logo wall.
- **Candidate headline:** “Clear intent. Checked changes. Accountable delivery.”
- **Composition:** a compact editorial interlude, with a readable sequence integrated
  through the same space. Let the spatial rhythm support the sequence without
  turning it into a dashboard, mandatory interactive tutorial or long process manifesto.
- **Transition:** finish with a concrete reason to inspect Apollo, rather than another
  generic capabilities list.
- **3D's eventual job:** make continuity and checkpoints legible. No conveyor, machine
  or abstract pipeline is committed yet; the explanation must work as plain HTML.
- **Evidence boundary:** distinguish documented policy from demonstrated practice.
  A candidate sanitized example is the portfolio foundation's retained
  [dev acceptance/recovery record and production addendum](https://github.com/benhammoush/portfolio/issues/8).
  It concerns the accepted 0.1.0 foundation, not the current local 0.3.0 composition.
  It does not prove identical enforcement across all projects. Select and approve
  the public example before final copy; never publish memory or raw conversations.

## 03 — Apollo

**Takeaway:** ability to reason about a complex system as a coherent whole, not just
assemble its visible interface.

- **Content:** the problem and users, the author's contribution, one understandable
  system-level decision, the tradeoff and approved evidence. Select the actual
  engineering story before writing a finished claim or choosing a scene.
- **Candidate headline:** “Keeping a complex system coherent.”
- **Composition:** the first substantial project reveal. Give approved evidence a
  focal role, with concise explanation and room for a public-safe overview. This
  should feel like entering the first project story, not viewing a decorative trophy.
- **Transition:** move from system-level coherence to the clarity of a user-facing
  information experience in Iris.
- **3D's eventual job:** explain relationships or reveal an approved project artifact.
  Avoid invented terminal walls, generic trading charts and private architecture
  reconstructed from operational notes. No object is selected yet.
- **Evidence boundary:** Apollo source remains private. Narrative, diagrams and video
  need disclosure/content approval. The previously approved video destination is
  `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7ox8NgQMjfM`, subject to that review; use click-to-load
  and a normal link fallback if selected. No profitability or live-performance claims.

## 04 — Iris

**Takeaway:** turning complex public-wallet and market information into a usable
exploration experience.

- **Content:** one actual supported user journey, the interface decisions that make
  it understandable, the author's contribution and accepted evidence/limitations.
  Choose current approved screenshots before proposing spatial objects.
- **Candidate headline:** “Making complex information usable.”
- **Composition:** give real interface imagery and its hierarchy priority; retain its
  colors. Compose imagery, explanatory HTML and the world together without putting
  the whole section inside a floating monitor or repeating the Apollo layout.
- **Transition:** shift from exploring information to reasoning about payment state
  and completion in the gateway.
- **3D's eventual job:** guide attention, reveal relationships or give the selected
  interaction spatial context. It is not a substitute for readable project content.
- **Evidence boundary:** remain within the existing discovery/public-wallet/watchlist/
  resilience/accessibility scope. The current glass instrument and both earlier
  abstract Iris installations are rejected proposals, not design requirements.
  Schematic traces are not actual UI or live data and should not define the next concept.

## 05 — Crypto gateway

**Takeaway:** treating a payment as an explicit, verifiable sequence of states—not
just a successful-looking checkout screen.

- **Content:** the active product's actual payment journey, the author's contribution,
  one important state/verification decision and the evidence and limits that support it.
  Include uncertainty/failure handling only as supported by current contracts.
- **Candidate headline:** “Making payment states explicit.”
- **Composition:** a focused progression with a clear beginning, state change and
  result, readable without interaction. The content should explain the technical
  responsibility before any visual metaphor is chosen.
- **Transition:** resolve the project sequence into a clear invitation to contact,
  without celebration loops or animation barriers.
- **3D's eventual job:** clarify state and causality where useful; avoid decorative
  coins, wallets, token rain and invented blockchain/network details.
- **Evidence boundary:** reconcile the current gateway spec/contracts and accepted
  evidence before final public copy. Do not transfer old 0x settlement/order/Shopify
  acceptance, chain/network support or production readiness to this chapter. No
  commercial certification, throughput, monetary or live-readiness claims are implied.

## 06 — Contact

**Takeaway:** a visitor can act immediately after understanding the work.

- **Content:** a brief invitation, approved email and ordinary navigation back to
  the projects/profile. Only add resume/social destinations when approved and real.
- **Candidate headline:** “Let’s build something reliable.”
- **Composition:** a calm, readable conclusion in the same world. The contact action
  is the focal point, with no new visual puzzle or competing spectacle.
- **3D's eventual job:** provide closure through composition, not another object or
  forced final animation. The mail link remains immediately usable.
- **Evidence boundary:** use `guillaumeammouche@protonmail.com`. Do not invent
  availability promises, response times or additional contact destinations.

## Checkpoint before object and animation concepts

Review the takeaway and content priority of each section, and whether the sequence
reads as one coherent portfolio rather than a feature list. Candidate headlines
are editable proposals, not already approved public claims.

Do not add detailed geometry to the current rejected installation as a shortcut.
